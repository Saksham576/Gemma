# What's working in animated YouTube Shorts (October 2026)

Research for the channel's first three Shorts. YouTube itself, and some sites, are blocked from this build environment, so this comes from web search results (sources at the end). I couldn't watch the videos myself.

## 1. Shorts first, or long-form first?

**Start with Shorts. Add long-form once a Short format proves itself.**

- Shorts are where new channels get discovered: they reach people who have never searched for you, and a new channel can break out with a Short just as easily as an established one. A viral Short can add 5,000–50,000 subscribers in days. [Subscribr](https://subscribr.ai/p/youtube-shorts-vs-long-form-growth), [wpreset](https://wpreset.com/how-to-grow-faster-on-youtube-in-2026-shorts-strategy-click-through-rate-and-watch-time-optimization/)
- Long-form earns roughly **10–15× more per view** ($2–8 CPM). [Subscribr](https://subscribr.ai/p/youtube-shorts-vs-long-form-growth), [upGrowth](https://upgrowth.in/youtube-shorts-vs-longform-roi-2026/)
- The recommended steady state is a mix, treated as one audience journey rather than two channels. [upGrowth](https://upgrowth.in/youtube-shorts-vs-longform-roi-2026/)

For an *animation* channel this matters more than usual: a 2–3 minute episode costs as much as 6–9 Shorts to make. Test characters and gags cheaply in Shorts, and turn the winner into an episode.

**The plan:** post the three pilots below, one every 2–3 days. Watch *Viewed vs Swiped away* (section 3), make more of whichever pilot wins, and once one character or gag type clearly works, make a 2–3 minute episode with it.

## 2. What the biggest animated Shorts have in common

The top of the Shorts charts is dominated by **wordless character comedy**:

- Monica Toy: cute characters, playful "funny and failed imitation" gags "that anyone can enjoy regardless of language", 2.2B views. [HitPaw](https://online.hitpaw.com/learn/most-viewed-shorts-on-youtube.html)
- BlobbyClips (#48 in the global weekly top 50, 291.5M views in one week) and Capybluh (2.5M subscribers, 2.3B views): a recurring character duo in short gags. [Tubefilter](https://www.tubefilter.com/2026/09/28/top-50-most-viewed-youtube-channels-week-of-09-27-2026/), [OutlierKit](https://outlierkit.com/channel/capybluh)
- 35 of the global weekly top 50 channels are mainly Shorts channels. [Tubefilter](https://www.tubefilter.com/2026/09/28/top-50-most-viewed-youtube-channels-week-of-09-27-2026/)
- Character cartoons in this lane deliver "quiet antics, quirky reactions … perfect 15- to 60-second stories" with warm, muted palettes. [OutlierKit / capybara-cartoon roundup](https://outlierkit.com/channel/capybluh)

**The formula:** one recognisable character, one everyday situation, one escalation, one twist, and no language barrier. Sak (watercolour, expressive face, squash-and-stretch) fits this exactly.

## 3. How the algorithm judges a Short

- Since March 2025 every play and every replay counts as a view. What drives distribution is **engaged views**: watching past the first few seconds, likes, comments, shares, and intentional replays. [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/youtube-shorts-algorithm/), [fliki](https://fliki.ai/blog/youtube-shorts-algorithm-explained)
- **The swipe-away rate is the most important signal.** Under 30 s, aim for less than 25% swiped away; top creators get 75–80% "viewed". If more than 70% swipe in the first 2 s, the hook is broken. [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/youtube-shorts-algorithm/), [easyviral](https://easyviral.ai/api/blog-md/youtube-shorts-algorithm-2026)
- **Watch-through above ~70%** gets aggressive promotion. [Shortimize](https://www.shortimize.com/blog/youtube-shorts-retention-rate)
- It works as explore then exploit: a seed audience first, then wider waves for days or weeks if they respond. [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/youtube-shorts-algorithm/)

## 4. Craft rules this implies

| Rule | Evidence | How the pilots apply it |
|---|---|---|
| **Hook in the first ~2 s** | "If you don't capture attention in the first two seconds, you've already lost" [Prapermedia](https://prapermedia.com/blog/make-viral-youtube-shorts/) | Frame 0 is already mid-tension, with a question the viewer wants answered (a ticking toaster, a wobbling tower, a cloud raining on one character) |
| **15–25 s for comedy** | Comedy clips do best at 15–25 s, and 15–30 s gets the highest completion [Piktochart](https://piktochart.com/blog/how-long-youtube-shorts/), [Toptal](https://www.toptal.com/creator/post/youtube-shorts-length) | Each pilot is about 18–20 s |
| **A visual change every 2–3 s** | Attention span on Shorts is roughly 3–5 s [fliki](https://fliki.ai/masterclass/tutorials/how-to-get-more-views-on-youtube-shorts) | One read per beat: setup, escalation ×2, twist, payoff, reset |
| **Make it loop** | Replays are "one of the strongest distribution triggers"; the end should point back into the opening [virvid](https://virvid.ai/blog/looping-structure-shorts-retention-2026), [faceless.so](https://faceless.so/blog/how-to-create-a-seamless-loop-video-1) | Every pilot's last action leads straight into frame 0 (exact-frame loop) |
| **It must work with the sound off** | A large share of short-form is watched muted [fliki](https://fliki.ai/blog/how-to-get-more-views-on-youtube-shorts) | Wordless gags read through acting alone, and sound adds to them rather than carrying them |
| **Sound design still pays off for listeners** | Well-timed whooshes, pops and dings make Shorts feel dynamic [Splice](https://spliceapp.com/blog/mastering-youtube-shorts-editing-guide/) | Cartoon foley on every action, plus a short music bed that loops with the picture |
| **Keep clear of YouTube's on-screen buttons** | The Shorts player covers the bottom ~20% (title, channel) and the right edge (like, comment, share buttons) | The action stays in the centre of the frame; nothing important sits in the bottom 380 px or the right 180 px |

## 5. The three pilots (each tests a different hook)

| Pilot | Hook type | Frame 0 | Twist | Loop |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Toast** | suspense / anticipation | Sak staring down a ticking toaster, sweating | The toast never comes down… until it lands on Sak's head like a hat | Sak pushes the lever again, back into the stare |
| **Tower** | tension / "don't fall" | A wobbling tower of blocks, with Sak adding one more | It finally balances, then a fly lands on top and it all comes down | The falling blocks land back into a wobbling tower |
| **Cloud** | absurd premise | A tiny rain cloud raining only on Sak | Sak uses the rain to grow a flower, which blooms and shields Sak; its pollen puffs into… a new tiny cloud | The new cloud is frame 0's cloud |

Compare them after 48–72 h on *Viewed vs Swiped away* and average % viewed. The winning hook type tells you what to make more of.
